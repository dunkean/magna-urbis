References verified online on 28 October 2026 using author, publisher or project pages. The local contribution is identified to distinguish influence, adaptation and validation. The papers are not copied into this site.

  1. Perlin, K. (2002). Improving Noise.

    Context for coherent gradient noise. The project uses 2D simplex noise; this paper does not describe that code exactly.

  2. Barnes, R., Lehman, C. & Mulla, D. (2014). Priority-Flood: An Optimal Depression-Filling and Watershed-Labeling Algorithm for Digital Elevation Models. Computers & Geosciences 62, 117–127. DOI: 10.1016/j.cageo.2013.04.024.

    The authors’ reference and repository for depressions and basins; locally adapted in erosion and drainage.

  3. Tarboton, D. G. (1997). A New Method for the Determination of Flow Directions and Upslope Areas in Grid Digital Elevation Models. Water Resources Research 33(2), 309–319.

    Facet-based direction and D∞ area partitioning; distinct from the final vector graph.

  4. Braun, J. & Willett, S. D. (2013). A Very Efficient O(n), Implicit and Parallel Method to Solve the Stream Power Equation Governing Fluvial Incision and Landscape Evolution. Geomorphology 180–181, 170–179. DOI: 10.1016/j.geomorph.2012.10.008.

    The family of implicit incision solvers. Magna Urbis bounds and interactive parameters remain local. Metadata verified in the author’s institutional GFZ repository; the publisher page was unavailable to the automated reader during that review.

  5. Barnes, R., Callaghan, K. L. & Wickert, A. D. (2021). Computing Water Flow through Complex Landscapes — Part 3: Fill–Spill–Merge. Earth Surface Dynamics 9, 105–121.

    Filling, spilling and merging depressions. The local module uses a simplified equilibrium balance rather than the entire published dynamics.

  6. Goslin, A. (2025, revised 2026). InfiniteDiffusion: Bridging Learned Fidelity and Procedural Utility for Open-World Terrain Generation. arXiv:2512.08309v4, 3 May 2026.

    Lazy, unbounded algorithm and Terrain Diffusion application. Magna Urbis computes a finite window with its own planner.

  7. Karras, T., Aittala, M., Aila, T. & Laine, S. (2022). Elucidating the Design Space of Diffusion-Based Generative Models.

    EDM: decomposition of choices in model design, preconditioning, training and sampling.

  8. Karras, T. et al. (2024). Analyzing and Improving the Training Dynamics of Diffusion Models.

    EDM2: magnitude control; context for network architecture, not evidence of urban quality.

  9. Song, Y., Dhariwal, P., Chen, M. & Sutskever, I. (2023). Consistency Models. PMLR 202, 32211–32252.

    Generation in one or a few evaluations, trained directly or distilled.

  10. Lu, C., Zhou, Y., Bao, F., Chen, J., Li, C. & Zhu, J. (2022). DPM-Solver++: Fast Solver for Guided Sampling of Diffusion Probabilistic Models. arXiv:2211.01095.

    Multistep high-order solver; the coarse stage samples with its second-order multistep form, as the teacher does, and its students shorten that run.

  11. Hinton, G., Vinyals, O. & Dean, J. (2015). Distilling the Knowledge in a Neural Network. arXiv:1503.02531.

    Training a small student on the outputs of a large teacher; the sense in which the decoder student is distilled. The student here regresses teacher outputs, not class probabilities.

  12. Dettmers, T., Pagnoni, A., Holtzman, A. & Zettlemoyer, L. (2023). QLoRA: Efficient Finetuning of Quantized LLMs. NeurIPS 2023.

    Source of the 4-bit NormalFloat (NF4) code; the compact base uses its sixteen values with an independent codec, for storage only.

  13. Burn, a deep learning framework in Rust (Tracel AI).

    Tensor library of the native neural engine, on wgpu; the browser engine replaced it with its own WGSL kernels.

  14. Galin, E., Peytavie, A., Maréchal, N. & Guérin, E. (2010). Procedural Generation of Roads. Computer Graphics Forum 29(2), 429–438. DOI: 10.1111/j.1467-8659.2009.01612.x.

    The anisotropic shortest-path family; the project has its own costs, classes and limits.

  15. Parish, Y. I. H. & Müller, P. (2001). Procedural Modeling of Cities. SIGGRAPH, 301–308.

    Comparative reference: global goals and local constraints for L-system urban growth.

  16. Chen, G., Esch, G., Wonka, P., Müller, P. & Zhang, E. (2008). Interactive Procedural Street Modeling. ACM Transactions on Graphics 27(3), article 103.

    Comparative reference: tensor fields and street-graph editing. No complete implementation of the solver is claimed.

  17. Vanegas, C. A., Kelly, T., Weber, B., Halatsch, J., Aliaga, D. G. & Müller, P. (2012). Procedural Generation of Parcels in Urban Modeling. Computer Graphics Forum 31(2pt3), 681–690.

    Reference for constrained urban subdivision; local cuts and frontage territories are a partial adaptation.

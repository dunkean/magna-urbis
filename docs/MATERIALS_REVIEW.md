# Atlas of terrestrial natural textures — Catalog for Neural Earth

> English translation of the [French original](MATERIALS_REVIEW_FR.md).

For a global procedural generator, approximately 15 major landscape families can be distinguished, with over 200 types and variants of visually recognizable surfaces. The aim is not to classify biomes but to ask:

> Looking at any place on Earth from above, what natural surface types, colors, structures, and patterns might be encountered?

The reference scale is aerial or satellite observation at 0.2–5 m/pixel, with patterns ranging from a few meters to several kilometers. A conventional classification such as ESA WorldCover distinguishes only 11 land-use classes at 10 m/pixel, insufficient for the desired visual diversity.

Three things must also be distinguished:

- Surface texture: sand, granite, grass, canopy, ice, mud, water, and so on.
- Spatial pattern: stripes, spots, polygons, ripples, branching, bands, fractals, and so on.
- Geomorphological context: plain, slope, summit, valley, coast, delta, plateau, depression, and so on.

A rocky coast and a granite summit can share a material while requiring different distributions, structures, and transitions.

## 5. Structuring the library in Neural Earth

Rather than 200 independent shaders, build a combinatorial system around several layers:

```text
Geographical context
  Climate · altitude · slope · aspect · geology · hydrology · coastal proximity
1. Material
  Soil, rock, sand, water, ice, vegetation
2. Morphology
  Dune, valley, cliff, beach, slope
3. Pattern
  Bands, patches, cracks, ripples, networks
4. Cover
  Trees, grass, blocks, moss, algae
5. State
  Wet, dry, snowy, flooded, frozen
6. Local variation
  Grain, color, density, roughness
Final texture and auxiliary maps
  Albedo · roughness · normals · height · material masks · vegetation density
```

| 100–10 m | Large patterns: dunes, meanders, forest fronts, valleys, bars |
| 10–1 m | Organization: trees, blocks, ponds, gullies, crevasses |
| 1 m–10 cm | Local texture: grasses, stones, cracks, vegetation details |
| 10–1 cm | Microtexture: grains, rock roughness, soil, leaves, moss |

The finest levels are generally not directly identifiable in conventional satellite imagery. They must be synthesized consistently with higher-level materials and processes.

### Catalog sizing

For an initial advanced version, indicative library sizes are:

| Component | Indicative library size |
| --- | --- |
| Fundamental materials | 30–50 |
| Visual material variants | 100–150 |
| Spatial pattern generators | 20–30 |
| Geomorphological structure generators | 30–50 |
| Vegetation cover models | 20–30 |
| State and season modifiers | 15–25 |

These figures are not from a standardized scientific classification; they are software sizing estimates based on the catalog.

For Neural Earth, biomes should not directly select textures. First determine local geomorphology, substrate, and surface dynamics, then distribute materials and patterns compatible with climate and hydrology.

The same climatic context could then produce a sandy plain, basalt plateau, sandstone canyon, sebkha, or alluvial valley with truly different satellite appearances, without artificially multiplying biomes.

# Render v2: procedural satellite environment

Render v2 evaluates deterministic local environmental appearance from world metres, physical slopes and annual climate. It does not modify the DEM, drainage, inference or generation settings. Its season slider is independent of the legacy materials. Changing the date updates uniforms in the map and perspective, without regenerating terrain, forest fields or projected textures.

The Terrain Bench exposes one **Render v2** entry in Style. Its **Diagnostic** selector beside Season switches between the satellite appearance, substrate, moisture, vegetation, and snow/ice views. Existing `style=render-v2-*` links still restore the corresponding diagnostic; the season and camera remain unchanged.

Shared equations with Neural Earth separate mineral substrate, ground cover and canopy. The substrate diagnostic includes correlated dark, brown, limestone and ferric red provinces at 180 km and 690 km. Terrain position and bounded kilometre scale variation refine humidity. In the satellite view, the existing Render3D open ground and canopy anchor the material mean at every footprint. Zoom reveals filtered normals, albedo residuals and roughness rather than switching the ground palette between 6 and 1.5 m/pixel. Map, perspective and raster evaluate the legacy material once, retain open ground and leaf-on canopy before snow, and apply the V2 calendar afterwards. The canopy's seasonal colour is the ratio between V2's current snow-free canopy and its leaf-on reference, applied to the retained canopy mean; snow is then added once. Fine analytic bands are footprint filtered without variance renormalization. Herbaceous sward has a small amplitude; it does not use miniaturized crowns.

The satellite ground also receives the shared bounded mineral-province tint. The ratio of its mineral colour to the neutral mineral province is clamped to [0.6, 1.4] and mixed with unit RGB at weight `0.35 * (rock + (1-rock)*(1-grass)*(1-organic)*0.35) * (1-snow)`. Thus exposed rock changes by at most 14% per display channel, bare non-organic soil by at most 4.9%, and grass, organic matter and snow attenuate the correction. This is a fixed-mean material correction across close zooms; the existing province appearance contrast fades only between 600 and 2400 m/pixel. Sediment flats, beach and snow are applied after the tint, and canopy uses its separate retained mean. The source open-ground guide is captured before sediment for this purpose; legacy Render/Render3D composition remains unchanged.

The regional annual climate is adapted to northern summer/winter by an amplitude of 6 + 0.5 max(0, 10 - annual Celsius), following the existing continental growing-season proxy. This is an appearance calendar, not planetary meteorology. Potential stands remain fixed while deciduous opacity and shadows respond to leaf fraction. Existing riparian envelopes, physical water and applied occupation/buildings constrain vegetation. No agriculture is generated.

Snow cover is the maximum of seasonal cover and an annual retention floor. The floor is `smoothstep(2.5, -2.5, warmest + exposure) * smoothstep(0.12, 0.7, supply) * (1 - smoothstep(0.45, 1.25, slope))`, with exposure in Celsius, dimensionless annual precipitation supply, and physical slope magnitude. Here `smoothstep` accepts either endpoint order. The annual floor uses the warmest local season after the same lapse and exposure adjustment as seasonal snow; the unfiltered climate/slope calculation excludes stand noise and river-bank moisture. Thus perennial-cold, supplied, gentle terrain retains a mantle in summer, while dry sites and steep slopes can remain exposed. This is an appearance retention proxy, not an observed glacier mask, accumulated snow mass or glacier dynamics. The generated shared GLSL applies the same equation to map, perspective and raster output.

The compositor applies the existing filtered ground signal after mixing open ground and forest litter. Individual crowns retain their own texture and use the macro terrain normal rather than inheriting mineral micro-slopes. Snow-covered litter returns to the seasonal snow surface. Natural stands use the population-weighted three-state response described below; occupation and narrow bank galleries retain the existing macro guide. The retained beach mask is converted back from the legacy 0.8 colour-blend weight to physical coverage: it excludes grass, gallery stands and forest reveal even when snow covers the sediment. The separate legacy low-flat sediment mask also survives the close-ground reveal, without removing trees from those flats. Potential herbaceous cover reduces exposed low-flat sediment in V2; the physical beach remains a separate later overlay. Perspective evaluates coastal openness from the same retained terrain so that this mask is also available there.

Map and perspective use the same BRDF and exposure as Render3D. City Generator evaluates relief lighting directly and has no separately baked macro-light factor: an additional normalization against a flat surface incorrectly darkened its composed forest material and has been removed. Terrain slope, crown normals, cast shadow, occlusion and roughness variance remain in the material response. Legacy low-flat sediment is applied after the unresolved canopy, while resolved crowns stand above that sediment. Centimetre ground bands remain filtered at metre footprints.

The unresolved V2 canopy additionally uses the shared intrinsic-crown BRDF integral. Its 19-altitude table integrates the existing broadleaf/conifer normal distributions, roughness 0.9 and crown occlusion, storing diffuse/specular responses and ambient means. The ratio to the flat BRDF uses the same two actual light directions and lighting strength; strength zero returns unity. Only the unresolved canopy mean receives this ratio, weighted by `1-snow`, before canopy snow. The guide for actual resolved crowns is unchanged. Map, perspective and retained raster pass both applied light directions and strength; the raster's optional defaults match the existing default lighting. This intrinsic preintegration is separate from ecological coverage; the three-state compositor supplies that coverage for natural stands.

Resolved floor modulation first bounds the normalized continuous form to [-1, 1], then applies an amplitude of 0.04 for soil, up to 0.06 for exposed rock and 0.006 for snow. Herbaceous modulation similarly bounds its unit form before multiplying by 0.025. Clamping directly to these small amplitudes incorrectly converted much of the continuous form into two plateaus. V2 display conversion keeps positive values above one until its final land-lighting shoulder, before water composition; Render3D retains its original conversion. For display RGB peak `p > 0.8`, the shared shoulder maps the peak to `0.8 + 0.2 * (1-exp(-(p-0.8)/0.2))` and scales all RGB channels by the same ratio. Peaks at or below 0.8 are unchanged. This is a small approximation for the hybrid display/BRDF path, not ACES or a full scene-linear colour pipeline.

Water is composed last. A substrate/depth response varies with coverage. Freshwater, moving river and seawater freeze thresholds differ. Inland depth inferred from coverage is an appearance proxy, not measured bathymetry. Compact pack uses weakly warped 1.2 km adjoining opaque plates and thin leads. Marginal pack selects detached opaque plates from 2.6 km parent cells, with 470 m subdivisions in selected parents and independent 215 m XYZ displacement. A concentration blend from 0.68 to 0.90 keeps the dense pack rigid. Shapes use the distance gap F2-F1 of 27 jittered 3D lattice candidates, adapting [Worley's cellular basis (1996)](https://doi.org/10.1145/237170.237267), rather than its full Poisson sampling. Temperature and shallow-water depth influence concentration; depth is a coastal appearance proxy, not a geometric coast-distance query. Footprint-dependent edge widths filter the plates; coverage, brightness and edge tint converge to a calibrated area mean from 140 to 450 m/pixel. This mean is approximate, not an exact per-cell integral. Icebergs require nearby cold coastal land sources, grouped once from retained terrain into at most eight spatial bins with bounded 80 km influence. Their eroded, lobed cellular silhouettes have varied sizes up to several hundred metres, white plateaus, darker facets, cyan submerged edges and directional cast shadows from the same cell. Source strength selects density rather than making the ice translucent; depth attenuates the source without excluding deep fjords. From 115 to 290 m/pixel these blocks converge to an approximate mean coverage. They have no simulated calving or currents. [NSIDC stages and structure](https://nsidc.org/learn/parts-cryosphere/sea-ice/science-sea-ice) and [NASA's IceBridge atlas](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/11143) are appearance references, not implemented dynamical models. Regional maps lack monthly discharge: no river is guessed dry from local aridity.

The marginal correction selects whole opaque fragments with binary eligibility and confines cyan edges to visible ice, avoiding translucent whole plates and cyan networks in open water. Antialiasing remains on physical fragment edges and unresolved coverage. The compact pack retains its adjoining plates and narrow boundary leads; closed noise-isocontours were removed from its interior because they read as artificial contour drawings at 5 m/pixel. Iceberg shapes are independent of these corrections.

The actual GLSL is reused by map, perspective and raster paths. Diagnostics use the same evaluator, including the actual composed pack/iceberg coverage. Dry land returns before cellular water shading. Raster exports evaluate the same canopy material at their own resolution; they do not promise identical individual crown pixels at different camera footprints. The full coastal export keeps the computed sea and ice pixels: land-colour extension and land-only clipping are bypassed for v2. The initial perspective placeholder is transparent for v2, until the actual water/ink texture arrives.

The following three-state compositor addresses that coverage mismatch. For natural stands without an occupation raster or a narrow bank gallery, the canonical forest field supplies canopy cover `c`, height, needleleaf fraction and stand tone. The existing within-stand density model gives `d=forestDensity(c,kappa)` and stand share `s=c/max(d,0.001)`. With available land fraction `a=(1-beach)*(1-water)` and leaf fraction `l`, weights are `1-a*s` for open ground, `a*s-a*c*l` for exposed litter and `a*c*l` for crowns. Each state is lit separately, then its response is converted back to the existing linear BRDF working space and area-weighted. Open ground keeps its existing material occlusion; litter multiplies it by 0.72, as in the resolved compositor; crown roughness is 0.9 and occlusion is already included by the intrinsic-crown integral. Stand tone passes through the same `forestCrownColor` function as actual crowns before preintegration. Averaging albedo and occlusion separately would lose their covariance and incorrectly darken exposed ground.

The original crown-resolution reveal blends this statistical response into the existing resolved response; it is not forced to one in the displayed result. The resolved-material calculation exposes its original reveal to the final shader, and resolved crown coverage retains that same factor for water/raster alpha. The mean never evaluates the near stand-threshold pattern. No new tree projection stage, texture noise or exposure gain is introduced. The final population sample uses only the physical LOD `max(log2(footprint/cell),0)`. An experiment added the existing bounded stand-boundary prefilter `2*(1-forestReveal(footprint))`; it was removed after the progressive clearing-profile comparison below to avoid extra softening. Box mips preserve the population channel's mean; reconstructing nonlinear density and stand share from the filtered channels is an approximation. Occupation/building exclusions and narrow gallery contracts remain on their existing compositor path.

The final V2 field therefore uses bounded thinning rather than a threshold-shaped opening. With the same natural target `c` and unchanged 100–400 m disturbance score, `thinning=min(0.55,2*c/(1+exp(clamp(-score/1.15,-20,20))))`, and potential cover is multiplied by `1-thinning`. Symmetric, zero-bias scores preserve mean loss `c` before the cap; finite terrain bias and the cap make this an approximation. The disturbance itself retains at least 45% of potential cover before the separate user density control, so a disturbed cell does not turn into a new full clearing. Rock, bog, drought, treeline and sea exclusions can still produce genuine open ground through their existing independent models. Small canopy gaps remain in the resolved crown population. This changes stand density consistently at all footprints; it is neither a distant image blur nor a colour/exposure gain. The direct CPU adapter uses `c`; the canonical shader helper can recover that target from the inherited quantile parameter. Render3D keeps its historical threshold and field bytes. V2 completed-field and UI cache keys are versioned `bounded-thinning3`.

Compact marine pack retains the same 27 feature-site candidates and now uses `(d2²-d1²)/(2*length(B-A))`, the distance to the two nearest sites' bisector plane, instead of F2-F1 as lead width. Narrow nominal leads are integrated over the pixel footprint; subdued refrozen seams and filtered compression relief avoid a second dark network. This is a procedural cellular adaptation, not ice dynamics or geometric pressure ridges. Marginal pack, bergs and inland ice keep their previous field. The Euclidean bisector normalization is our derivation; it is not claimed as an equation from [Worley's cellular-texture paper (1996)](https://itn-web.it.liu.se/~stegu76/TNM084-2019/worley-originalpaper.pdf). Neural Earth's CPU 4096-point comparisons preserve those unaffected fields' four output channels exactly; nine actual decoder-30 m views at 40 km, 10 km and 5 m/pixel passed capture gates. These independent polar checks are documented in its `docs/design/render-v2-polar-review.fr.md` and do not establish ice dynamics or photorealism.

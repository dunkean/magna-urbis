# Underdark

The Underdark (*Outreterre*) is a world layer, not a relief: any relief, procedural or neural, can be carved into a cave system. In V2 it is the **World layer** control (`options.layer`): *Surface* or *Underdark*. Underdark sets `options.biome = 'underdark'`.

## Generation

The finished relief (coasts, sculpt and erosion included) goes through one Rust stage, `magna_urbis_terrain::underdark::carve` (`rust/crates/terrain/src/underdark.rs`), called by `TerrainGenerator::carve_underdark`. The browser engine exposes it as WASM `carve_underdark` and the local service as the `underdark` operation of `/terrain/relief`. The bridge (`rust/bridge/terrain.ts`) runs it once per relief when `TerrainSettings.underdark` is set, then the ordinary hydrology runs on the carved surface.

The relief is read as the floor of the underworld. It is not inverted.

1. **Floor.** The relief, lifted above zero. Its range is compressed above 600 m, which keeps the drainage.
2. **Tunnels.** The drainage of the floor becomes the corridors. Their radius grows with the upstream area, swells into irregular river rooms, and is warped so that D8 diagonals become meanders.
3. **Caverns.** Basins and plains lower than their surroundings, shaped by a broad noise, cover the share of the map set by **Underdark cavern share** (`terrain.underdarkCaverns`, 0.2 by default; under it, corridors and galleries narrow too). Two to four of them are great halls, wide openings on the most cavernous ground with few pillars; elsewhere rock pillars stand inside.
4. **Galleries.** The zero crossings of a warped noise give a sparse labyrinth of dry passages.
5. **Clean-up.** Small openings close and small rock grains open. Every remaining chamber joins the main network by its cheapest passage (Dijkstra through rock), so roads and rivers can reach it.
6. **Walls.** The smoothed signed distance to the wall drives the rock height. Rock rises continuously from the floor of its nearest opening, starting two cells inside the rock so the display spline puts no step on the floor. Water falling on the rock drains into the nearest gallery.
7. **Side tunnels and chambers** (`underdark_sides.rs`, with **Underdark tunnels**). On a jittered lattice over the rock masses: dead ends, some forked, tunnels to a blind chamber, links between two galleries with their crest inside the rock, and chambers sealed in the rock, all a few metres wide with irregular sizes. Floors rise from the mouth, so they capture no river. A sealed chamber drains through a narrow low line in the rock (a fissure, not an opening).
8. **Lakes and silt.** Some caverns near a large river sink into an 8–18 m bowl, fewer with a lower **Lakes** setting; with the lakes off, no bowl is carved and every basin is silted. Then every closed basin of middling size (0.04 % to 2 % of the map, at least 4 m deep) stays closed and becomes an underground lake; all other basins, from small pits to huge drowned valleys, fill with sediment into flat cavern floors.

The output is the carved height and a **soft rock field**: 128 on the wall, ±96 per source cell, saturated inside a cell. Interpolated, it draws curved walls at any resolution. The region `caveMask` (0/1) remains for logic; the physical field `cave` (soft) feeds the GPU grounds, the wall polygons and the settlement raster (rock is `>= 128`).

## Tunnels

A tunnel is a narrow, long passage through a rock mass, unlike a cave: near-constant width, a purposeful line, two mouths. The amount is the **Underdark tunnels** parameter (`terrain.underdarkTunnels`, 0 to 3, default 1: about 4 lava tubes and 2 river passages on a 10 km map, 6 and 3 on a 40 km map; 0 gives none, nor any side tunnel or sealed chamber).

- **River passages** (terrain carve). Where a river (at least 8 tunnel heads of upstream area) crosses thick rock (no cavern or gallery within its radius plus two cells and one base radius) with no confluence, over at least 200 m and three corridor radii, its corridor narrows to a gut with no warp and no wall noise: half width a tenth of the corridor radius, 4 to 9 m (wider than a lava tube: it carries the river). The carve opens the river's own chain of cells (made 4-connected at each diagonal step, plus the cells within the half width when it exceeds a cell). The longest reaches are chosen, spread over the map. Each cross-section of the slot takes the lowest floor of the corridor it replaces, as a running minimum downstream, so the slot never dams the river: standing water never rises.
- **Lava tubes** (terrain carve, after the network is joined). The widest points of the chambers are hubs. Every rock run that the line between two hubs (at most 30 % of the map apart) crosses is a candidate, from the open floor before it to the open floor after it, when it is 200 m to 4 km long (at most a fifth of the map) and the way round between its two mouths is at least 2.5 times longer (bounded below by the hubs' network distances on a coarse grid). The candidates that save the most distance are carved, spread over the map: a smooth, gently sinuous tube, half width 3 to 7.5 m, with one or two short skylight rooms. The carve opens the 4-connected chain of cells under its centre line (a diagonal step also opens the corner nearer the line) and the cells within its half width. Its floor runs linearly from 2 m above the highest water level near its upper mouth to 2 m above the one near its lower mouth (at most a 15 % grade), so it drains to the lower mouth and captures no river.
- **Data.** `UnderdarkField::tunnels` (`kind` 1 lava tube, 2 river passage; half width; centre line mouth to mouth). The engine keeps them (`underdark_tunnels`, WASM `underdark_tunnels()`, local service `read` with `tunnels: true`) and the page reads them into `terrain.caverns.tunnels`.
- **Width.** Both kinds are guts drawn at their real width on every map size. The planning raster (rock mask: roads, water, towns) keeps whole open cells along the chain, so the passage stays connected and the river keeps its grade. The soft rock field the renderers read takes, around each gut, the distance to its centre line minus its half width, held to the carve (the gut's cells stay open, rock stays rock): the wall contour stands at the half width, about 6 to 15 m apart for a lava tube on a 10 km or a 40 km map. On a coarse grid the drawn gut follows the chain of cells (within half a cell of the line) and reads about 0.7 cell wide on diagonal reaches (about 25 m on a 40 km map). The relief keeps the plain wall distance: in 3D the rock rises from a cell beside the gut, so a gut is a narrow floor at the bottom of a trench a few cells wide.
- **Display.** Both kinds are open floor in the terrain field, so water, roads, towns, the GPU ground and the 3D trenches see them like any passage. The map styles add a discreet typed stroke (`render/caveTunnels.ts`): a dark basalt lining with a faint ropy centre for a lava tube, a pale ledge path beside the water for a river passage, both at the tunnel's own half width.
- **Names.** Each tunnel is named along its passage with the role `terrain.tunnel.<kind>` ("Lava Tube …", "Passage …"; « Tube de lave … », « Passage … »).
- **Dug tunnels** (roads stage, `rust/crates/core/src/settlements/roads/tunnelling.rs`). On an Underdark map, the roads of a culture that digs may cross cave rock (`Fields::is_rock`) when the way round is longer than the dug cost: one dug metre costs 5 open metres for duergar-hold and dwarven, 6 for gnomish, 12 for drow-enclave; other cultures never dig (`roads/tunnelling.json`, data). The search enters the rock only for those queries (a third cached move mask; single steps, no grade inside the rock, turns four times dearer: surveyed straight reaches). Bounds are checked on the found path, else the route is searched again without digging: two mouths on open floor, at most 900 m, at most a 10 % grade between the mouths, and a per-map budget of `RoadConfig.tunnels` (default 1) × 3 × sqrt(width / 10 km). Cave rock is not a crossing (`deck::spans` skips it): the dug stretches are `RoadSegment.tunnels` (`kind: 'dug'`), each carried on to the first sample whose cell and its neighbours are open floor so both mouths stand on the floor, which the page adds to `terrain.caverns.tunnels`: the road is drawn as a worked corridor (straight dressed walls, regular floor, portals at both mouths) over the rock, and named ("Tunnel …", « Tunnel … »). In 3D the raised rock hides the road between the mouths, and a framed dark opening is painted at each mouth on the rock face (`drawTunnelPortals`). The terrain field is not changed (no hydrology re-run).

## Consequences

- **Water.** The normal water chain runs: rivers flow in the tunnels and lakes fill the kept basins. Underdark water default: `condMode: 'none'`, since the carve already decided which basins stay (explicit settings win).
- **Settlements and roads.** Rock is class 2 at ground level: it cannot be built on or crossed. Towns settle on cavern floors and roads follow the tunnels.
- **Land use.** No climate forest. Natural cover gives moss and fungal beds (class 7) and bare floor (8). Farms are fungal farms, and they belong to the cave towns: no farm lines the roads across the cave floor and no farm hamlet stands apart. Isolated farms (placed farmsteads and farms for cave floor no town reaches) are capped at one per 500 inhabitants, at most 40 per map (`cave_farm_cap`, `rust/crates/core/src/landuse/rural.rs`); the floor no farmer reaches stays wild.
- **Cultures.** Choosing Underdark sets the main culture to `drow-enclave`, unless it is already `duergar-hold` or `myconid-colony`.
- **Names.** Summit labels are not generated, because rock masses have no summits. The widest caverns (local maxima of the distance to the rock) are named with the `terrain.cavern` role, so the generic word follows the display language ("Cavern …", « Caverne … »).

## Render

- **Satellite and 3D** (`rust/bridge/terrainRenderUnderdark.ts`, shared by the 2D map shader, its WebGPU twin and the 3D ground):
  - The cave floor is limestone, basalt and wet sediment, under a cold key light.
  - Near-black fractured rock carries a lit ledge at the wall and ambient occlusion onto the floor.
  - Moss, bioluminescent fungi and giant mushroom groves grow on the floor, with stalagmites and debris at close zoom.
  - Fungal farms (champignonnières) are the cultivated land of the cave towns (occupation class 13, `FUNGUS_BED`): organic patches, warped from the class share, divided into fields (44 m blocks) with their own row direction and spacing; rows of compost beds broken by cross paths, every fifth row a path, one cap size and colour per bed, a few beds glowing. The 3D farms use the same layout. The wild floor is bare rock, moss and damp tones; the giant caps of the groves are scattered individuals.
  - Water is dark and luminous at its edges.
  - There are no trees, snow, seasons or foam.
  - Towns are lit by the night lamps and cast a warm lantern glow on the cave floor (`uTownGlow`, 2D and 3D).
  - In 3D the sky is the black void of the vault.
- **3D cave** (`web/src/render/underdarkCavern.ts`, `web/src/ui/terrain3dFungi.ts`, `web/src/render/city3dMushroom.ts`; both 3D backends, compiled only for the Underdark):
  - *Section model.* Every rock mass rises as a wall to a roof level and is cut there: the roof follows the floor of the nearest opening plus a clearance that grows with the chamber (`effects.underdark.roof` is the clearance of a medium chamber, about 2 × in great chambers, up to 7 × under the dome of a great hall; a passage keeps a vault at least 1.5 × its half-width). The cut is a matte near-black section fill with a fine lighter line.
  - *Roof with a cutaway.* The roof is a real mesh at that level, cut away around the camera's focus (a circle that grows with the zoom, edged with the section band); seen from inside it is dark stone lit from below; ceiling stalactites go with it. Walking or flying keeps the whole vault overhead. Framing views look across the cave from a low angle, the eye under the roof where the chamber allows.
  - *Walls and light.* Walls carry strata, crevices and grain read along the wall, scree at their foot, a damp sheen near water, and darken towards the cut. No sun: a dim cool ambient (brighter in great chambers, darker in passages), the town lanterns as point lights that light the walls facing them, pools over the fungal clusters; the far cave dissolves into darkness, a warm haze around the towns. `effects.underdark.darkness` sets the ambient and how near the darkness comes. Buildings are lit by their lanterns and fade with the ground.
  - *Mushrooms.* Instanced lathes of one parametric family (parasol, dome, bell, cone, funnel, puffball, morel, club, wall bracket, stalactite) placed from the 2D fields: fungus forests of tree-sized parasols, domes and bells (10–34 m, under the roof) in the great humid chambers, with glades; the 2D giant caps as scattered individuals; lone mushrooms and small groups of two to five on the bare floor, mostly empty; the rows of the farms near the focus; shelves on wet walls; stalactites on the rim of the cut and under the roof. Towns, ways and water are cleared. A few glow softly (cyan-green, violet, amber). Built around the focus, nearest first, at most 32 000, in three levels of detail (full lathe near the focus and for large forms, six sides, a one-band cap for the small ones); `effects.underdark.fungi` widens the forests and the scatter (0 hides them).
  - *Myconid dwellings.* A `mushroom` massing volume is drawn as an amanita: a broad, nearly flat cap with pale warts overhanging its footprint (broader for a landmark), a deep rounded gill skirt below the rim, a ring on the stem; the body is the stem and keeps its doors and windows.
  - *Tilted map.* The single camera of the V2 (the 2D map that tilts, `web/src/render/world/`) shows the same cave on material looks: its relief is the section model (`cavernSectionHeights`, also on paper looks), the ground and the town take the cave's light and dissolve into its darkness under a black sky (`cave.ts`, `terrainShader.ts`, `buildingsShader.ts`), and a `cave` layer draws the roof (cut away around the orbit target, whole overhead when walking or flying) and the mushrooms (`caveLayer.ts`). No modelled tree grows there. Dug road tunnels keep the road's own width and bare floor.
- **Map styles.** The overlay draws the exact iso contour of the soft field: marching squares at 0.5 with no blur, so it meets the GPU cut. The underdark palette (`render/biomes.ts`) and the fungal marks (`render/underdark.ts`) apply. Cultivated fungal beds (farm plots, fungal land use, town gardens) share one look (`fungalBedLook`): a dark compost bed of the cave floor's tones, small irregular mushroom caps and a rare faint glowing spore head, no pale fill and no geometric glyph. Vector styles keep their own bed tone and a light farm-ink outline; on the satellite looks the farm plots take the compost of the floor shader with no outline.

## Tools

- `cargo run -p magna-urbis-terrain --example underdark_probe -- <seed> <width> <relief> <n> <out.ppm>` writes a shaded preview and a slope crop.
- `node scripts/render_board.mjs --recipe-patch '{"options":{"biome":"underdark","culture":"drow-enclave"},"runtime":{"backend":"native"}}' …` shoots a board. Add `--neural` with a neural recipe.

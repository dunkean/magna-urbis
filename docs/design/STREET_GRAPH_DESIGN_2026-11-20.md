# Streets as a connected graph: design and roadmap

## 1. Summary

1. Keep the field, noise, grid frames, radial bearings, terrain routing and cultural choices that draw a town. Change how an accepted line enters the town.
2. Give every junction one position and an angularly ordered set of arms. Keep ordinary bends as samples inside an edge.
3. Seat an arriving arm on its host in the same operation that splits the host. Preserve the host’s curve, interpolated width and identity through the split.
4. Give a street a persistent identity and an ordered chain of directed edges. Give frontages, reservations, doors and edits references into that chain.
5. Start inside one existing network-cleaning boundary and continue exporting polylines. Retire a repair in the first delivery.
6. Move quarter cuts, hierarchy and access onto the graph before changing design policy. Then admit deliberate offset junctions, bridge approaches, gate passages and short stair flights at construction.
7. Derive blocks from the ground-level graph plus land boundaries. Generate the two sides of a street and its junction corners once. Fix capacity decisions before changing the lot cutter.
8. Send the same topology to the editor, Canvas, WebGL and 3D. Keep curb construction, stroke styling and level-of-detail decisions in the renderer.
9. Admit one measured change at a time. Every accepted version must generate a complete town, house its households and pass the replay gates below.
10. At completion, whole-town re-noding, geometric reservation remapping, display-time mouth snapping and repeated subtraction of street ribbons disappear. Allocation, terrain checks, routing and wall fitting retain their distinct jobs.

## 3. Target model

**Design.** `Layout` owns one `StreetGraph`. Only the graph mutates accepted urban street geometry. A `StreetDraft` may contain a free polyline while the field, router or editor is proposing a change. Discarding a draft has no effect on streets or land.

| Rust entity | Contract |
|---|---|
| `Node` | Stable `NodeId`, canonical metric `Point` in `f64`, owner, elevation/level port and ordered incident `HalfEdge`s. Kinds cover junction, designed hub, land gate, water gate, bridge end, quay/landing, street end and town exit. A node can carry multiple attachments, e.g. a bridge end at a land gate. A water gate can own a navigation opening without any traversable street arm. Degree follows incidence. |
| `Edge` | Stable `EdgeId`, two node handles, interior shape samples in drawn order, width at both ends and each sample, class/mode, rank, role, purpose, grade summary, support/level and owner. End coordinates come from the nodes. Store edge length and station offsets. Keep the first and last nonzero tangent for arm order. Distinguish a through diversion from a local frontage lane even when their surfaces look alike. |
| `StreetChain` | Stable `StreetId`, optional name, ordered directed edge references and original station lineage. A street may curve through several junctions or change width, role or support. Two routes may share one physical edge. For a genuine break, retain explicitly separate pieces with lineage. A named market street with a 15.6 m gap gains a traversable link only if the intervening market actually supplies one. |
| `FrontageRef` | `StreetId`, directed source-station interval, side and resolved edge interval(s). Doors have a station and a side. A reservation owns its particular contact with a block. Splitting a host redistributes intervals exactly. Removal explicitly reassigns or invalidates dependants. |
| `Face` | Ground boundary cycles of directed edge sides, including typed **non-traversable** boundaries for quarters, shore, wall reserves and the town envelope. A buildable block is the usable land within such a face after offsetting by street widths and clipping other reserves. Store holes, ownership and attributed frontage intervals. A navigable water surface, elevated deck and ground block occupy separate layers. |

**Mutation contract.** `insert(draft, start, end)` accepts a node handle, a host edge and station, or an intentional new end. Query segment bins only over the changed corridor. Order all comparable same-level intersections by source station and stable ID. Split the arrival and all hosts in one transaction, interpolate widths, resolve exact overlaps into shared runs and publish the new arms and dirty land together. Reusing a node changes only the admissible approach at its mouth. If seating changes a proposed quarter split, validate the children on the seated geometry before committing either one.

`merge_nodes`, `split_edge`, `remove`, `move_node` and `set_width` use the same transaction. A merge checks levels, sector order, barriers and the intervening land. Width changes dirty both adjacent faces and receiving junctions. Removal cascades through chain intervals, frontage, gates and dependent faces. Refuse a removal that strands required housing or breaks a required through route until a replacement is in the transaction. Store reverse references. Expose `arms(node)`, `sectors(node)`, `incident_faces(edge)`, `nearest_host(point, level, mode)` and reachability by travel mode. Use incremental bins for nodes, segment bounds and barriers. A deletion can split a component, so invalidate that component and traverse it on the next connectivity query. Union-find alone is insufficient.

**Invariants.** Every live arm has a reciprocal end. Every chain is continuous within each declared piece. Widths are finite and positive, samples ordered and edges of positive length. Every traversable same-level crossing has a shared node. Separate levels meet only at an explicit transition. Road and pedestrian reachability are distinct. Every occupied street system reaches the town network through a legal connection, including a declared off-map or neighbouring connection where applicable. An isolated anchored fragment does not qualify. Resolve an infeasible district before assigning households. Physical water requires an admitted support or crossing. A curtain requires a passage that authorizes that mode. Verify the changed neighbourhood on mutation and audit the whole graph in replay tests.

**Character.** One `street_improvisation` share, clamped to `[0,1]`, default `0.25`, governs eligible tidying decisions. A retained offset T consists of two junctions and a real intervening edge. A retained double has two distinct axes, their actual ends, and an account of the intervening land, which may be a verge or retaining wall. A radial hub may have six or more arms. Draw once per eligible decision. Dryness, grade limits, gate authorization and reachability do not participate in that draw. Remove overlapping tidying controls as their call sites migrate. **VERIFIED starting point:** the Rust share already exists for gate fronts, while `street_frontage` can force all qualifying parallel runs to merge. `mod.rs:768,1028`, `fortification_gates.rs:219`, `network_site_options.rs:9`, `rust/bridge/urbanConfig.ts:173,203`.

**Structures and limits.** A deck reserves two abutments and a through approach on each bank. Other arms join beyond its landing. A terrace has a support level and sampled ground elevation. Stairs connect compatible landing ports, rise in short flights and can share a pedestrian route with level landings and a separate contour road. A quay has a water side and land-side access. A harbour basin can be inside the circuit or served through a land gate outside it. Regional roads and rural tracks outside urban ownership keep their regional representation. The town owns the clipped inner portion and binds its exit to the regional segment and station. A track that serves town frontage participates in the graph under its own access rules.

## 4. Determinism, parallelism and identity

**VERIFIED basis.** The cutter already shares a read-only base and major-street bins, forks per quarter, speculates in parallel and commits in quarter order. On EO 100k it recut two of 109 quarters in one round. Preserve that advantage. `quarters.rs:679,3222,3573,3657,3815`, `edits.rs:123`, `runs-keep1/s-eo-100000-smooth/stderr.log:213`.

**Design.** Workers read a frozen graph revision and emit local drafts, proposed land splits and a short read set. Record the winning host **and station**, occupied sectors, local intersections and relevant barrier answers. An equal nearest distance with a different host is a changed answer. Commit in stable macro-owner, quarter-lineage, recursive-cut order. Revalidate only reads touched by earlier commits. Recut a conflicting quarter with the same stream against the accepted prefix. A single-thread build follows that same order directly. No worker acquires a town-wide geometry lock.

A macro border owns its nodes. On a shared quarter boundary the canonical boundary owner, chosen by ordered quarter IDs, owns the station. Both quarters request that port. Across towns the earlier claim owns the binding. A later town exports its exit and claimed host station. Compose a split at that station in the regional connection table without regenerating the earlier town. Test that traversal crosses the seam in both directions.

Use dense `u32` arena handles internally and opaque stable IDs externally. Derive generated IDs from the town key, producing operation, owner lineage and semantic slot. A split node derives from the host lineage and crossing operation. Child edge IDs derive from lineage and bounding node IDs. Persist user-created IDs with the edit. Moving a bend preserves its edge and chain IDs. A structural redraw retires only replaced lineages and reports unresolved edit targets. Use canonical tie-breaking, collision checks and fixed operand order in reductions. Never derive identity from allocation order, rounded XY or a mutable vector index.

Keep existing field streams. Fork optional tidying from `(town seed, owner, decision kind, ordered participant IDs, decision slot)`. A rejected candidate, a different thread count or a remote edit consumes no other decision’s draw.

Rebuild web, bench and desktop consumers. Regenerate old saved projects or reject their version with a clear message. Old seeds, edit hashes, URLs and APIs may break.

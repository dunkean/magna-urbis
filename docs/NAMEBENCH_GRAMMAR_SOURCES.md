# Namebench grammar: scope and sources

The country pools are modern geographic samples, not language-pure or historical lexicons. A profile's `family` is a compatibility bridge, not a claim that its language is the same as that family. Urban and feature labels use the finer profile. Names supplied as complete opaque labels are never parsed or redecorated. In particular, a geographic source name's grammatical gender, case, internal article, and the pronunciation of an initial H cannot be inferred reliably from its spelling; the caller can provide traits when known.

The following primary references informed specific implemented rules:

- [Académie française, elision](https://www.academie-francaise.fr/y-til): French elision before vowels and mute H; we require explicit annotation for mute H. Article contractions are handled separately.
- [Treccani, Italian prepositions](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/di-preposizione_%28La-grammatica-italiana%29/): *di* combines with definite articles. We contract visible or explicitly supplied articles and do not guess grammatical gender.
- [Türk Dil Kurumu, apostrophe](https://tdk.gov.tr/icerik/yazim-kurallari/kesme-isareti/): Turkish proper-name suffix spelling is context-sensitive. The map uses fixed separate compound labels, and does not invent a case suffix on the destination.
- [Geospatial Information Authority of Japan, toponymic guidelines](https://www.gsi.go.jp/ENGLISH/page_e30065.html): Japanese geographic names and romanized generic terms. Our simple romanized joins are a display convention, not full phonological analysis.
- [New Zealand Geographic Board, geographic generic terms](https://www.linz.govt.nz/products-services/place-names/new-zealand-geographic-features/about-nga-ahuatanga-matawhenua-o-aotearoa-new-zealand-geographic-features): identifies geographic type words such as *awa*, *roto*, and *maunga*. The Māori profile uses these as separate generics; other words and constructed names have not been vetted by speakers.
- [BGN/PCGN Chinese romanization](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5ab4deb6e5274a1aa593343d/ROMANIZATION_OF_CHINESE.pdf): modern Chinese geographic-name romanization; country-pool spellings are preserved rather than automatically converted.

## Authored conventions and limits

Fantasy profiles and the explicitly constructed grammars are game-world naming conventions, not reconstructions or authoritative translations. The Roman profile uses Latin-inspired generic terms and does not claim reconstructed historical place-name syntax. The Iroquoian, Pueblo, Plains, Mississippian, Pacific Northwest, Australian and Nubian tables contain authored labels and do not claim to represent the actual languages of those peoples. More broadly, a single vocabulary cannot represent every language in a large geographic region: for instance, the southern African and west African tables are only stylized Zulu- and Yoruba-influenced conventions. Ancient Egyptian, Mesopotamian and other historical tables use approximate simplified forms, without claiming historical grammar. Spellings across other profiles are lightweight game conventions and need review by language specialists before being represented as authentic. These limits do not alter or transliterate original country-source names.

The small term-based duplicate guard recognizes only explicit word boundaries and selected hyphens. It is not a general morphological parser and intentionally does not remove a guessed ending from an opaque name. Urban craft, quarter and worship labels are generic; they do not imply a full historical semantic lexicon. Invariant connectors intentionally avoid guessed case inflections. Additional language coverage requires independently sourced terms and native-speaker review.
